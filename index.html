1Read the text

The flower chafer, also known as the golden chafer, is a beautiful beetle with a shiny golden or metallic-green appearance and a somewhat flattened body. It is often seen on flowers in summer.

Most beetles open their hard wing covers, called elytra, when they fly. The golden chafer does not need to do this. Its back wings slide out from under the elytra and unfold through small cuts at the edges. This lets it take off quickly and fly fast. It is a nimble flier and can hover briefly above flowers.

Although it looks metallic, the beetle has no pigment. Its colour comes from light reflecting from microscopic layers on its body. This effect is called iridescence. It may help the beetle blend into green surroundings and escape predators.

Golden chafers are herbivores. Adults eat nectar, flowers, and overripe fruit. Their larvae live in soil and feed on decaying wood and plant material.
